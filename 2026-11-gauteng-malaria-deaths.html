Gauteng malaria deaths nearly triple, health department warns travellers

The province recorded 17 malaria deaths between January and September, up from six in the same period last year. Most were linked to travel in southern Africa.

By Rolivhuwa · 17 November 2026 · 1 min read

The Gauteng Department of Health has warned of a sharp rise in malaria cases and deaths. The department said on Thursday 12 November that the province recorded 768 cases and 17 deaths between January and September 2026. In the same period of 2025 it recorded 554 cases and six deaths. That is a 38.6% rise in cases.

For the whole of 2025, Gauteng recorded 666 cases and seven deaths.

Most deaths linked to travel

Of the 17 deaths, 15 involved people who had travelled to countries in the Southern African Development Community (SADC) region. Mozambique accounted for about 59% of the deaths, according to reports of the department’s figures. The department has previously said that Gauteng’s malaria cases are imported from high-risk areas.

What residents should do

The department urges people travelling to malaria-endemic areas to take precautions, and to seek medical attention immediately if they develop malaria-like symptoms. These include fever, headache and muscle or joint pain, and can look like flu. Anyone who falls ill after travelling should tell the health worker where they have been.

Sources: SAnews, EWN, TimesLIVE and Sunday World reports.
