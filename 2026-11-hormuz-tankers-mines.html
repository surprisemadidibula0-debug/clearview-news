Iran says three more tankers hit mines in Strait of Hormuz

Iran’s navy and Revolutionary Guards say three tankers caught fire on Saturday after striking mines. The US says it has cleared the main routes, and Reuters could not verify the claims.

By Rolivhuwa · 15 November 2026 · 2 min read

Iran’s navy and Revolutionary Guards say three oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday. Reuters said it could not immediately verify the reports.

According to Iranian state media, one tanker hit a mine in the eastern part of the strait on Saturday morning, and two others struck mines later in the day. The Guards said the two later vessels were trying to slip through a route that Tehran has declared prohibited.

The US disputes the picture

The US military says it has cleared the main routes through the waterway of mines. Iran has restricted navigation in the strait and has reported several fires or explosions on ships there in recent weeks, often saying the vessels struck mines.

The human cost

The UN’s International Maritime Organization says at least 25 seafarers have been killed in and around the strait since the US-Israeli war on Iran began on 28 February. The agency tracked 136 security incidents involving vessels in Hormuz and the wider Middle East between 1 March and 11 November, 35 of them in October.

UN official Khaled Khiari told the Security Council on Friday that the situation must not be allowed to become the new normal.

Drone claim in Bahrain

Separately, the Revolutionary Guards said on Sunday that they launched six drones at a US air-defence radar at the Al-Rifaa helicopter base in Bahrain. There was no immediate confirmation from Washington or Manama that the radar was hit.

Bahrain’s Defence Force said its air defences intercepted and destroyed several aerial attacks before they reached their targets. Two days earlier, the Guards made a similar claim about a US early-warning radar in Bahrain. The US military denied it, saying it intercepted two Iranian drones without damage.

Sources: Reuters, AFP and Al Jazeera reports.
